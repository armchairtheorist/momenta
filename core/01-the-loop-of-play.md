# The Loop of Play
*Momenta Core Rules · Prologue · Welcome · Chapter 01*

Momenta is two games that take turns: a **tactical wargame** and a **visual novel**. One is played on a grid, in rounds, with every Vigor point counted; the other is played in scenes, in conversation, with dice only when the outcome is in doubt. Neither half is the "real" game. The story happens in both, and the whole design is built so that what you do in one half matters in the other.

## The shape of a campaign

A campaign is a series of **missions**. A mission is a sequence of **encounters** and **scenes**, separated by **interludes**, and it ends in **downtime** before the next mission begins.

```
                          ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   MISSION begins ──────► │  ENCOUNTER ──► INTERLUDE ──► SCENE ──► ENCOUNTER … │ ──► DOWNTIME ──► next MISSION
                          │  (combat)      (rest,        (narrative   (combat)   │    (recover,
                          │                short scene)   play)                   │     advance)
                          └────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Missions** are the unit of story. Each has objectives, a **mission clock** that measures how much time the party has, and a **campaign difficulty** that sets the stakes. See **The Mission Loop**.

**Encounters** are the tactical half. Combat runs in rounds on a grid; sides alternate turns; you spend **Vigor** to act and you win by achieving the encounter's objectives, not by clearing the map. See **The Shape of a Fight** and the rest of Moment 4.

**Scenes** are the narrative half. The GM frames a situation, you say what your character does, and you roll only when success is uncertain and failure would be interesting. Your **Threads** (who you've been) and **Bonds** (who you care about) are the tools of this half. See **Narrative Actions**.

**Interludes** are the breath between encounters: a short rest, a chance to recover, and room for one meaningful scene — a conversation by the fire, a vigil, a confession. See **Interludes**.

**Downtime** is the long rest between missions: full recovery, advancement, and the personal projects and relationships that grow when the pressure is off. See **Downtime**.

## What crosses the line

Two resources belong to both halves, and they are what make the halves one game.

**Momentum** is the party's shared pool. It is earned by achieving objectives, seizing dramatic moments, and playing your character truly — in combat and in scenes alike — and it is spent on the biggest swings in both: Limit Breaks in a fight, Momentous Feats and Developments in a scene. See **Momentum**.

**Resolve** is personal grit. It pays for pushing yourself, for invoking the Bonds that drive you, and for recovering from what a mission does to you. Spend it in a scene and you have less for the fight; spend it in the fight and you have less for the scene. That tension is deliberate. See **Characters at a Glance**.

**Bonds** are the third bridge. They are written and grown in scenes, and once they are strong enough, they change what you can do in combat. See **Bonds** and **Bonds in Combat**.

## Reading the loop at the table

You are in **combat** when the GM calls for initiative and puts the grid on the table. You are in **narrative play** the rest of the time. The GM decides when to switch, and the switch is always a story beat: the ambush springs, the villain flees, the dust settles. When it does, Momentum you've built carries across, Threads and Bonds you've invoked are still true, and the mission clock keeps ticking.
