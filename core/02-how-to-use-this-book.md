# How to Use This Book
*Momenta Core Rules · Prologue · Welcome · Chapter 02*

This book is the complete rules of Momenta. It is written concisely and assumes you have played a tabletop roleplaying game before. Content — archetypes, masteries, equipment, spells, Limit Breaks, enemies, and ready-to-play characters — lives in the separate **catalogs**, which this book points to but does not repeat.

## Reading paths

- **Playing a character:** read the Prologue and Moments 1 through 4 in order. Moment 1 is the engine, Moment 2 is your character, Moment 3 is the story half, Moment 4 is the fight. Skip Moment 5.
- **Running the game:** read everything. Moment 5 is yours, and the chapter **Running the Game** is the best place to start.
- **Returning to a rule mid-session:** go straight to the chapter — each is one page in the online rules and one section in the book — or to **Appendix B · Glossary**, which lists every game term with a pointer to its home.
- **Learning the game for the first time:** a separate Quickstart, distilled from these rules and shipped with the pregenerated characters, is the gentler door. This book is the full house.

## Conventions

- **Bold** marks a game term where it is defined and where a rule invokes it. Terms in **Title Case** are the names of specific things (Vigor, Hindered, Grunt, Basic Attack); lowercase words describe kinds of things (a maneuver, a condition, a stat block).
- Attributes and cores are written in capitals: **POW, AGI, REA, INS, PRE, LUCK** and **BODY, MIND, SOUL**, along with **SPD, SEN,** and **SIZE**.
- Dice are written as `3d6`; bonuses and penalties to your dice pool as `+2d` and `-1d`; results as successes, with `0s / 1s / 2s / 3+` naming the bands a result can land in.
- "See **Chapter Name**" points to another chapter. Nothing is stated twice: if a rule seems to be missing from where you are, the pointer tells you where it lives.
- Blockquotes labeled **Design Note** explain why a rule is the way it is. They are for curious readers and for anyone modifying the game; you can skip them and lose nothing.
- The **GM adjudicates** is a rule, not an evasion. Where the book says so, the decision belongs to the GM on purpose (see **Running the Game**).
